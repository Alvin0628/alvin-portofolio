Key takeaway

DAPK1 dominates twice: it has the strongest p-value in the scan and the top SHAP importance in the model. Yet three loci alone reach 68.4% accuracy, and the model recovers only 53% of vulnerable dogs while catching 86% of resistant ones. The loci are informative, but not sufficient on their own.

Reading it carefully

With λ = 1.69, the scan's p-values are inflated, so the top SNPs are best read as ranked candidates rather than confirmed causal variants. A 76-dog test set also carries roughly ±10 points of sampling uncertainty on accuracy.

Summary

What the three loci can and cannot support.

Conclusion

Three loci are candidate markers, not a diagnostic signature.

DAPK1, TRPM3 and EYA4 carry the strongest single-marker associations on chromosome 1. However, cancer is a multifactorial, polygenic disease, and three single-nucleotide polymorphisms cannot serve as a standalone predictive or diagnostic signal. These loci are best interpreted as hypothesis-generating candidates for further investigation.

Polygenic architecture

Many variants, small effects

Oncogenesis reflects the cumulative contribution of numerous variants of small effect, interacting with age, sex, reproductive status and environmental exposure. Even the strongest marker, DAPK1, has a marker R² of 0.207, leaving roughly 79% of phenotypic variance unexplained by that SNP alone.

Predictive sufficiency

Insufficient sensitivity

On the held-out set, the model classified 19 of 40 vulnerable dogs as resistant, a false-negative rate of 47.5%. Sensitivity for the vulnerable class was 0.53 against 0.86 for the resistant class. Such performance would be inadequate for screening or clinical risk stratification.

Confounding and inflation

Interpret p-values with caution

A genomic inflation factor of λ = 1.69 indicates systematic inflation of the test statistics, consistent with residual confounding such as age or population stratification. Association p-values should therefore be regarded as exploratory rather than confirmatory.

Scope and next steps

Limits of this analysis

One chromosome, 378 animals, a binary phenotype and a single train-test split of 76 dogs. Reproducibility and clinical relevance would require:

  • a genome-wide scan adjusted for age and sex
  • correction for relatedness and population structure
  • cross-validation and calibrated risk estimates
  • replication in an independent cohort
