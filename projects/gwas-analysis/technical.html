How the analysis works

Scan chromosome 1, extract the strongest loci, then model them and explain every prediction.

Scan

TASSEL association test on 26,383 SNPs.

Extract

Genotypes at the three top SNPs for 378 dogs.

Model

XGBoost on one-hot genotypes, stratified 80/20 split.

Explain

SHAP for the cohort, LIME for single dogs.

Problem framing

Many SNPs, few dogs

26,383 markers against 378 dogs, with a nearly balanced label split. Scanning is cheap; the real question is how much signal survives when only a handful of loci are kept.

The interpretability layer

Explain the model, not just score it.

SHAP ranks which genotype states push predictions toward cancer vulnerability across the whole test set. LIME then breaks a single dog's prediction into its five strongest contributions, including the dogs the model gets wrong.

Statistics

Association scan

Tests each SNP on its own and ranks it by p-value. It finds where the signal is, but says little about how the loci combine.

Machine learning

XGBoost on three loci

Uses the three loci jointly, weights the imbalance between classes, and shows through SHAP and LIME how each genotype moves a prediction.

Genomic inflation

λ = 1.69

Median-based inflation factor computed from the TASSEL p-values. Values near 1 mean a clean test; 1.69 means observed statistics run well above expectation, a sign of confounding such as age or population structure.
